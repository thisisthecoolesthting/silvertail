Best Joint Supplements for Senior Cats: Evidence-Based Options

By Amelia Keller · Senior Editor

Published May 28, 2026

Choosing the right joint supplements for senior cats can feel overwhelming. There’s a sea of products making big promises, but not all of them are created equal. As cats age, they often experience joint pain and stiffness due to osteoarthritis. Identifying the right supplement, and understanding the science behind it, can significantly improve their quality of life. This guide breaks down the evidence-based options, helping you make informed decisions for your feline companion.

Understanding Joint Pain in Senior Cats

Unlike dogs who often loudly display their discomfort, cats are masters of disguise when it comes to pain. Subtle changes in behavior are often the first signs of joint issues.

  • Reduced Activity: Your cat might sleep more, play less, or hesitate before jumping.
  • Changes in Grooming: Difficulty reaching certain areas can lead to a matted or unkempt coat.
  • Litter Box Issues: Pain getting in and out of the litter box can cause accidents outside the box, often mistaken for behavioral problems. See our guide on Feline cognitive decline: what it looks like at the litter box.
  • Irritability: A normally affectionate cat may become withdrawn or even aggressive when touched.

If you notice these signs, a visit to the vet is the first step. They can diagnose osteoarthritis and rule out other potential causes. Remember, this article is not a substitute for veterinary advice.

Key Ingredients in Effective Joint Supplements

Once you’ve confirmed your cat needs joint support, it’s time to explore supplement options. Here’s a breakdown of the most common and well-researched ingredients:

Glucosamine and Chondroitin

This combination is a staple in joint supplements and works synergistically. Glucosamine is an amino sugar that helps rebuild cartilage, while chondroitin sulfate inhibits enzymes that break down cartilage.

  • How it works: Glucosamine provides the building blocks for new cartilage, while chondroitin protects existing cartilage from further damage.
  • Dosage: While there isn’t a universally agreed-upon dosage for cats, a common starting point is around 50-100mg of glucosamine and 25-50mg of chondroitin per day, adjusting based on your cat’s weight and response. Always consult with your veterinarian for specific recommendations.
  • Considerations: Look for supplements with high-quality, bioavailable forms of glucosamine and chondroitin, such as glucosamine hydrochloride and chondroitin sulfate.

Omega-3 Fatty Acids

These essential fatty acids, particularly EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid) and DHA (docosahexaenoic acid), possess potent anti-inflammatory properties.

  • How it works: Omega-3s reduce inflammation in the joints, alleviating pain and improving mobility.
  • Sources: Fish oil is the most common source, but krill oil and algae-based supplements are also available.
  • Dosage: The recommended dosage varies, but a general guideline is 20-40mg of combined EPA and DHA per kilogram of body weight daily.
  • Considerations: Choose fish oil supplements that are purified to remove heavy metals and other contaminants. Store fish oil in the refrigerator to prevent rancidity. Krill oil may be a good alternative for cats sensitive to fish oil.

Green-Lipped Mussel (GLM)

This shellfish from New Zealand is a rich source of omega-3 fatty acids, glucosamine, chondroitin, and other beneficial compounds.

  • How it works: GLM provides a comprehensive approach to joint health, combining anti-inflammatory and cartilage-building properties.
  • Dosage: Dosing varies depending on the concentration of the GLM extract. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions carefully.
  • Considerations: GLM can be more expensive than other supplements, but its comprehensive nutrient profile may make it a worthwhile investment.

Other Ingredients

  • Hyaluronic Acid: This substance is a major component of joint fluid, acting as a lubricant and shock absorber.
  • MSM (Methylsulfonylmethane): This sulfur-containing compound has anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties.
  • Turmeric/Curcumin: Curcumin, the active ingredient in turmeric, is a potent anti-inflammatory agent. However, it’s poorly absorbed, so look for formulations with piperine (black pepper extract) to enhance absorption.

Choosing the Right Supplement: What to Consider

With so many options available, selecting the best joint supplement for your cat requires careful consideration:

  1. Consult Your Veterinarian: This is the most crucial step. Your vet can diagnose the specific cause of your cat’s joint pain and recommend the most appropriate treatment plan.
  2. Read Labels Carefully: Pay close attention to the ingredients, dosages, and potential side effects.
  3. Consider the Form: Supplements come in various forms, including liquids, chews, capsules, and powders. Choose a form that your cat will readily accept.
  4. Look for Third-Party Testing: Reputable manufacturers will have their products tested by independent labs to ensure quality and purity. Look for seals of approval from organizations like the National Animal Supplement Council (NASC).
  5. Start with a Low Dose: Begin with a lower-than-recommended dose and gradually increase it to the recommended level, monitoring your cat for any adverse reactions.
  6. Be Patient: It can take several weeks or even months to see noticeable improvements in your cat’s joint health.
  7. Consider Palatability: If your cat is a picky eater, choose a supplement with a flavor they enjoy.

Beyond Supplements: A Holistic Approach to Joint Health

While joint supplements can play a vital role in managing osteoarthritis, they are most effective when combined with other supportive measures:

  • Weight Management: Maintaining a healthy weight is crucial to reduce stress on the joints. Work with your vet to develop a weight-loss plan if your cat is overweight. Our guide on Feeding an arthritic dog who gained weight from inactivity has tips that apply to cats as well.
  • Environmental Modifications:
    • Provide soft, comfortable bedding, such as an orthopedic bed, to cushion joints.
    • Ensure easy access to food, water, and the litter box. Consider low-sided litter boxes for easier entry and exit.
    • Use ramps or steps to help your cat reach favorite perches or furniture.
  • Gentle Exercise: Encourage gentle exercise, such as short play sessions, to maintain muscle strength and joint flexibility. Avoid high-impact activities that could exacerbate joint pain.
  • Diet: Consider a diet formulated for senior cats, often lower in calories and higher in protein to maintain muscle mass. Some senior diets also include added joint support ingredients.
