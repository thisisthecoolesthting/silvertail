Supplements for Senior Dogs with Cushing's Disease: What's Safe & Effective?

By Amelia Keller · Senior Editor

Published May 28, 2026

Cushing’s disease in senior dogs presents a unique set of challenges. The hormonal imbalance can lead to a range of symptoms, from increased thirst and urination to hair loss and muscle weakness. While medication prescribed by your veterinarian is the cornerstone of treatment, many pet owners explore supplements to help manage these symptoms and improve their dog’s quality of life. But with so many options available, it’s crucial to understand which supplements for senior dogs with Cushing’s disease are safe, effective, and worth considering.

Understanding Cushing’s Disease and Its Impact on Senior Dogs

Cushing’s disease, also known as hyperadrenocorticism, occurs when the adrenal glands produce excessive cortisol. This can be caused by a tumor on the pituitary or adrenal gland, or, less commonly, as a result of long-term steroid use. In senior dogs, the symptoms can be particularly debilitating, impacting their mobility, energy levels, and overall well-being.

Common symptoms of Cushing’s in senior dogs include:

  • Increased thirst and urination (polyuria and polydipsia)
  • Increased appetite
  • Pot-bellied appearance
  • Hair loss (alopecia), often symmetrical
  • Muscle weakness and lethargy
  • Skin issues, such as thinning or darkening of the skin, and increased susceptibility to infections
  • Panting excessively

It’s important to differentiate between Cushing’s symptoms and typical signs of aging. While some overlap exists, the severity and combination of symptoms often point towards Cushing’s. A proper diagnosis from your veterinarian is essential before considering any treatment plan, including the use of supplements.

Supplements to Consider (and Those to Avoid)

Navigating the world of supplements for Cushing’s disease can be confusing. Many products claim to “cure” or “treat” the disease, which is misleading and potentially harmful. Remember, supplements should be used as supportive care, alongside veterinary-prescribed medication, not as a replacement.

Supplements with Evidence-Based Benefits

While research specifically on supplements for Cushing’s in dogs is limited, some show promise in managing related symptoms or supporting overall health. Always consult with your vet before introducing any new supplement.

  • Lignans: Lignans, particularly those derived from flaxseed hulls, have shown potential in managing Cushing’s disease by helping to regulate cortisol production. Some studies suggest they may inhibit the enzyme responsible for converting certain hormones into cortisol. Look for products containing standardized lignans, and be sure to follow dosage instructions carefully. Why we like it: Lignans are often recommended by vets as a complementary therapy and are generally well-tolerated.

  • Melatonin: Melatonin is a hormone that regulates sleep-wake cycles and has antioxidant properties. Some veterinarians recommend melatonin for Cushing’s disease because it may help regulate the adrenal glands and reduce cortisol secretion. It can also help with sleep disturbances, which are common in senior dogs with Cushing’s. Important note: Use only pure melatonin, not formulations with xylitol, which is toxic to dogs.

  • Milk Thistle: Milk thistle contains silymarin, a compound known for its liver-protective properties. Cushing’s disease and its associated medications can put a strain on the liver, so milk thistle may help support liver function. Look for standardized extracts containing a high percentage of silymarin.

  • Omega-3 Fatty Acids: Found in fish oil and flaxseed oil, omega-3 fatty acids can help reduce inflammation and support skin health, which are often compromised in dogs with Cushing’s. Choose a high-quality fish oil supplement that is purified to remove heavy metals and other contaminants.

  • SAMe (S-Adenosylmethionine): SAMe is another supplement that supports liver health. It can help protect liver cells from damage and promote detoxification. SAMe is available in various forms, so it’s important to choose a product specifically formulated for dogs and follow the recommended dosage.

Supplements to Approach with Caution or Avoid

  • Adrenal Glandular Supplements: These supplements contain extracts from animal adrenal glands. While some proponents claim they can “balance” adrenal function, there’s no scientific evidence to support their use in Cushing’s disease. In fact, they could potentially worsen the condition by further stimulating cortisol production. Avoid these products.

  • Ashwagandha: While ashwagandha has adaptogenic properties and may help with stress, its potential effects on hormone levels in dogs with Cushing’s are not well-understood. It’s best to avoid ashwagandha unless specifically recommended by your veterinarian.

  • High-Dose Vitamin C: While Vitamin C is an important antioxidant, excessive doses can cause gastrointestinal upset, particularly in senior dogs. There’s no evidence that high doses of Vitamin C are beneficial for Cushing’s disease.

Diet Adjustments to Complement Supplements

Diet plays a critical role in managing Cushing’s disease symptoms. Choose a high-quality, balanced diet formulated for senior dogs. Consider these dietary adjustments:

  • High-Quality Protein: Maintaining muscle mass is crucial for senior dogs, especially those with Cushing’s, which can cause muscle wasting. Ensure the diet contains a sufficient amount of high-quality protein from sources like lean meat, poultry, or fish.   • Limited Phosphorus: Cushing’s can sometimes affect kidney function, so a diet with controlled phosphorus levels may be beneficial.

  • Moderate Fat: While fat is important for energy, excessive fat can contribute to weight gain, which is already a concern with Cushing’s.

  • Highly Digestible Carbohydrates: Choose foods with easily digestible carbohydrates to minimize digestive upset.

  • Fresh, Clean Water: Due to increased thirst, constant access to fresh, clean water is essential.

Monitoring and Adjusting the Supplement Plan

Introducing supplements for senior dogs with Cushing’s disease is not a one-size-fits-all approach. Regular monitoring and adjustments are crucial to ensure safety and effectiveness.

  • Start Slowly: Introduce one supplement at a time to monitor for any adverse reactions.

  • Keep a Journal: Track your dog’s symptoms, energy levels, appetite, and any changes you observe after starting a new supplement.

  • Regular Veterinary Checkups: Schedule regular checkups with your veterinarian to monitor your dog’s overall health and adjust the treatment plan as needed. Blood tests are essential to monitor cortisol levels and liver function.

  • Be Patient: It may take several weeks or months to see noticeable improvements with supplements. Don’t get discouraged if you don’t see results immediately.

By carefully considering the potential benefits and risks of various supplements, working closely with your veterinarian, and monitoring your dog’s response, you can create a comprehensive plan to manage Cushing’s disease and improve your senior dog’s quality of life. Remember, supplements are just one piece of the puzzle. A holistic approach that includes medication, diet, exercise (as appropriate), and loving care is essential for providing the best possible support for your beloved companion.
