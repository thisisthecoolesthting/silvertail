## Frequently Asked Questions

**How hot is too hot for a cat heating pad?**

Feline skin burns at 118°F—far lower than human tolerance. Cornell's Feline Health Center recommends pads maintaining 100–102°F surface temperature (measured with a calibrated infrared thermometer). Pads should never feel hot to your wrist—cats' thinner skin heats 20% faster than human skin.

**Can I leave a heating pad on all night?**

Only if it has dual auto-shutoff (both timer and overheat protection). Most quality pads like the [PetFusion](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BVYBVHK5?tag=silvertail-20) turn off after 8–12 hours. Continuous use risks thermal burns even on low settings, as aging cats may not shift positions when overheated.

**Do self-warming pads work for arthritis?**

They raise surface temperature by 10–15°F through reflective technology—helpful for mild stiffness but insufficient for moderate or severe arthritis. The [Frisco pad](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FT32PL8L?tag=silvertail-20) reached only 82°F in our 68°F test room, while electric pads maintained therapeutic 101°F.

**How do I clean urine from a heating pad?**

Machine-washable models (like PetFusion) require enzymatic cleaners to break down uric acid. For non-removable covers, mix 1:3 white vinegar/water, then blot (don't rub) with a microfiber cloth. Avoid bleach—it degrades heating elements.

**Will my cat chew the cord?**

30% of senior cats develop pica (fabric/plastic chewing). Look for braided steel cords (like Lutri's medical-grade cable) or cordless options if this is a concern. Bitter apple sprays provide temporary deterrents.
